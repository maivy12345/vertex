Earned Value

Reading CPI and SPI: what the numbers are actually telling you

Earned Value Management compresses a project’s health into two numbers. The Cost Performance Index (CPI) is earned value divided by actual cost; the Schedule Performance Index (SPI) is earned value divided by planned value. Both sit around 1.0 when things are on track.

A CPI of 0.9 means you are getting 90 cents of work for every dollar spent — a 10% cost overrun if the trend holds. An SPI of 0.85 means you have completed 85% of the work you planned to by now. Together they tell you not just that you are behind, but whether the problem is productivity, sequencing or scope.

The power of the indices is forecasting. Estimate at Completion divides the budget by CPI to project the final cost if current performance continues. It turns a vague worry into a number leadership can act on.

The discipline that makes this work is honest earned value — claiming progress only for work genuinely complete and accepted. We measure conservatively, on purpose.
