Schedule control

Why your project needs a baseline before it needs a Gantt chart

Vertex Capital Projects

A schedule without a frozen baseline is just a picture of optimism. The baseline is the contract between the plan and reality — the fixed reference every future update is measured against. Without it, “we’re behind” has no meaning, because there is nothing to be behind.

A good baseline does three things. First, it captures the agreed logic: what must finish before what, and why. Second, it loads resources and cost so the schedule can drive Earned Value, not just dates. Third, it is signed off and frozen, so that changes become visible as changes rather than quietly absorbed.

The most common failure we see is a live schedule that drifts continuously with no baseline to compare against. Progress looks fine every week because the finish line keeps moving. By the time the slippage is undeniable, the recovery options are expensive and few.

Our advice is simple: invest the time to build and freeze a logic-driven baseline before mobilisation peaks. It is the single highest-leverage controls decision on any project.
