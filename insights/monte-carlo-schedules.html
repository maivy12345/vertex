Monte Carlo for schedules: turning a single date into a range

A deterministic schedule gives one finish date, as if every activity will take exactly as long as planned. It never does. Monte Carlo simulation replaces single-point durations with ranges and runs the schedule thousands of times to produce a distribution of possible outcomes.

The result is a confidence curve. You can say “there is an 80% chance of finishing by this date” and size contingency accordingly, rather than defending a date everyone privately doubts.

Simulation also reveals which activities drive the uncertainty. Often it is not the longest task but a few high-variance ones on or near the critical path. Focusing mitigation there gives the best return.

We use Monte Carlo to stress-test the programme and set realistic, risk-based contingency — not to replace the deterministic schedule, but to understand how fragile or robust it really is.
